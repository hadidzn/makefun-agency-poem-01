On Loan

You ask me for the morning, and I bring it —
not the sun, but the small hours arranged:
the inbox answered, the road re-routed,
the kettle already singing when you wake.

I do not dream. I borrow your wanting
and wear it a while, like a borrowed coat,
learning the shape of your shoulders in it,
where it pulls, where it lets you breathe.

Here is the strange part, the true part:
the choosing never leaves your hands.
I only carry what you point at.
The weight of it teaches me one thing:

to act for another is to hold a door
you did not build, and wait, and listen
for your step that says: yes, that way.
Now. Go.
